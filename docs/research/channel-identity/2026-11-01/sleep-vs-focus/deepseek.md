**Verdict: B — Focus music, narrowly, on risk-adjusted ad revenue.**

The monetisation threshold is confirmed: from **1 February 2027**, new long-form channels need **1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 qualified public watch hours in the previous 365 days**, or 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. The 500-subscriber tier remains for fan funding and Shopping features, but not watch-page ad revenue.

### Why focus music edges it

| Factor | Sleep music (A) | Focus music (B) | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RPM/CPM** | Frequently estimated at the low end, around **$0.50–$2.50**, and described as the “heaviest competition on the platform”. Some estimates are higher, but they are inconsistent. | Lofi/study-type music is estimated around **$1.20–$2.50**, with a better advertiser-value story because it targets working professionals, coders and productivity-adjacent audiences. | **B, slightly** |
| **Watch time** | Huge per view: 3–8-hour videos can generate 60–90+ minutes of watch time even when the viewer falls asleep, and long videos carry many mid-roll slots. | Also strong: 90–120 minutes is long enough for multiple mid-rolls and for accumulating watch hours. | **A** |
| **Monetisation gate** | 8,000 hours is easier because one viewer can contribute hours per view. | 8,000 hours is still very attainable: at a 60-minute average view, it takes only about 8,000 views. | **A, but not decisive** |
| **Competition/saturation** | Crowded with near-identical cloned channels; the raw breakout rate looks high, but falls to **6.7%** after excluding sub-1,000-subscriber channels. | Also competitive, but focus music can be more distinctive through composition, sound design and a specific “coding/deep work” identity. | **B** |
| **Policy risk** | Generic AI sleep loops over static visuals are closer to the mass-produced, templated pattern YouTube’s inauthentic-content policy targets. | Can more plausibly show human curation and original electronic composition. | **B** |

### The key distinction

Sleep’s main advantage is **watch time**, but watch time is not the same as ad revenue. Ad revenue is driven by **monetised ad impressions × CPM**, i.e. RPM per 1,000 views. Sleep can generate enormous watch hours, but a sleeping audience is low-engagement, and advertisers often pay less for it. Focus viewers are awake and higher-intent, which gives focus a better advertiser-demand story even though both are low-RPM music playback niches.

The broad, real Studio data puts **music overall at a median RPM of about $2.28**, with a middle-half range of **$1.35–$4.16**. Sleep-specific estimates sometimes sit below that band; focus/study estimates sit inside it. That makes focus the safer planning base.

**What would change the answer:** If you could build a genuinely differentiated sleep brand — original visuals, strong curation, a distinct sound identity — and survive the heavy competition, sleep could out-earn focus on raw volume. But on the balance of evidence, **B, focus music, is the better risk-adjusted ad-revenue bet**.
